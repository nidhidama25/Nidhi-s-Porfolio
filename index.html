I started in support.
I found patterns.
Now I investigate why products
behave the way they do.

Most analysts start with data. I started with people complaining. Working in B2B SaaS support, I kept hearing the same problems — not because the product was broken, but because nobody was asking why users got stuck in the first place.

So I started writing SQL queries. Not because anyone asked me to. Because the patterns were too loud to ignore.

Chapter I

Origins.

2023

the support desk.

Data & Support Analyst at Udyog Software. B2B SaaS ERP environment. I was closing tickets — but also reading the data behind them. Why did 40% of onboarding queries come from the same three screens? Nobody had asked that question before me.

2024

the first queries.

SQL became the first language I trusted. I started writing queries nobody had asked for — mapping where users got stuck, where they came back, where they gave up. The patterns nobody was naming were the patterns hiding the answers.

2025

the frameworks.

Started building original analytical frameworks instead of borrowing off-the-shelf ones. The first was a brand archetype system applied to 25 Indian D2C companies — five archetypes, 415 posts, a custom purchase-intent metric. The 44% archetype-mismatch rate became my first published finding.

2026

the investigations.

Three case studies underway, each structured as an editorial investigation. A fintech teardown on Slice. A churn diagnostic on synthetic SaaS data. A marketplace cold-start analysis applied to restaurant partners. The point was never to build dashboards — it was to ask the questions nobody else was asking.

Chapter II

Investigations.

Each one is a question I went looking for an answer to. Methodology in the open. Findings on the record.

01

Instagram as a D2C growth engine.

The question
Do Indian D2C brands actually match their content to what their audience wants — or are they all chasing the same playbook?
The evidence
415 posts. 25 brands. A custom Share-to-Like Ratio metric as a purchase-intent proxy. An archetype framework with five categories.
The finding
44% of brands run the wrong archetype for their audience. Educator content beats Entertainer on SLR by 11 points. The follower count tells you less than the share rate.

→ Currently extending with a LangChain classifier that scores any Indian brand against the framework in real time.

02

The patterns inside churn.

The question
Why do SaaS users actually churn — and which ones could have been saved if anyone had seen them sooner?
The evidence
Synthetic event-level data across a B2B SaaS cohort. Survival curves, activation fingerprinting, revenue-weighted churn modeling, expansion revenue analysis.
The finding
Most "churn" concentrates in customers who never crossed an activation threshold inside the first 14 days. The metric to track isn't churn — it's activation depth.
SaaS · Survival analysis · Activation Read the investigation →
03

A health monitor for marketplace partners.

The question
Which restaurant partners are about to churn — and what could the platform have done about it 60 days earlier?
The evidence
Olist marketplace data, repositioned as a Swiggy partner study. Cohort survival analysis, churn risk scoring, a LangChain-powered natural-language summary layer.
The finding
Partners who don't complete their first three weeks of orders churn at 3× the rate of those who do. The real intervention window is 21 days, not 90.
